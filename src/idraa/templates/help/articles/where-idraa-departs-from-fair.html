Why this page exists

The authors of FAIR warn that most attempts to change the model — adding a branch, deleting one, changing a relationship, or adding weighted values — broke it, and that anyone who changes it must be “prepared to defend” the change and have it “tested and evaluated in an open forum” (Jack Jones & Jack Freund, Measuring and Managing Information Risk, 2nd ed., Elsevier, 2026, p. 42).

Idraa does change the model in a few places, and fills in numbers the standards leave blank in several more. This page lists every such place in plain language, says which are the standard’s and which are Idraa’s, and points to the defence. The full technical register, with code locations and tests, is published in the public repository.

What counts as a departure

Each item below carries one label:

  • Departure — a branch or relationship differs from what FAIR or FAIR-CAM prescribes. These are the ones the warning is about.
  • Added relationship — the standard describes an effect only in words; Idraa gave it a formula.
  • Calibration — a number the standard does not supply. Idraa says whether it is cited, estimated, or a convention.
  • Implementation choice — the standard gives the meaning but no formula; Idraa chose one and tests it against the meaning.
  • Not FAIR — reporting derivations that are not part of FAIR at all, kept outside the risk maths; the attribution table carries a modelled-estimates disclaimer on screen and its “not FAIR-grounded” note in the help, the rest are documented here.

The FAIR risk tree

Secondary loss happens on every loss event (departure). FAIR gives secondary loss its own frequency: the chance that a loss event also triggers fines, lost business, or reputational damage. Idraa has no such probability; the secondary loss range you author is added to every simulated event. If they would follow only some loss events, scale the range by how often they follow; set it to zero only when they are negligible, never because they are unknown. This is the one item on this page whose defence is incomplete: it keeps the average roughly right when you discount, but it smooths away the “nothing, or a lot” shape of conditional losses. Restoring the branch as an optional probability is tracked as issue #173.

You author Vulnerability directly (estimation level, allowed by FAIR). FAIR defines Vulnerability as the chance a threat’s capability beats the asset’s resistance, and lets you estimate at either level. Idraa asks for the inherent, control-naive Vulnerability and applies your controls in the simulation, so control credit is never counted twice.

Aggregate runs add up independent scenarios (implementation choice). FAIR does not say how to combine scenarios. Idraa sums their annual-loss samples with no correlation between them, which understates the tail when several scenarios share a cause. No portfolio-finance correction is applied. Within a single event, Primary and Secondary Loss are likewise drawn independently and summed, which understates the per-event tail for scenarios whose loss is split across both; the library’s share rubric was tuned knowing this.

Distribution choices

Your high estimate is a ceiling (calibration convention). Idraa reads your low and high as the 5th and 95th percentiles of a right-skewed lognormal, then samples from a bounded PERT whose top is that 95th percentile. The alternative, clipping a lognormal at a cap, piles the cut-off tail into a spike at the cap. The cost is a lower expected loss than the unbounded curve would give, accepted because the removed tail was not a plausible outcome for a single organisation.

Catastrophic losses are capped at organisational capacity (added relationship + calibration). Entries flagged catastrophic keep the heavy-tailed lognormal, but each loss component (primary, secondary) is bounded at one year of revenue by default, when revenue is recorded; with no revenue there is no cap and no substitute. FAIR has no capacity concept; the multiplier of one is a convention.

A default loss dispersion of σ = 1.7 (calibration convention). When nobody pins a spread, loss magnitude uses a round number sitting between the published industry reads for narrower and wider conditioning. No published statistic exists at the single-firm, single-scenario level this describes.

Several experts are pooled as a mixture (implementation choice). FAIR prescribes no pooling arithmetic. Idraa keeps each expert’s fitted distribution as its own component rather than averaging the ranges first, which would erase their disagreement.

Library loss figures are a sector envelope split by forms of loss (calibration). FAIR names six forms of loss but gives no arithmetic for building a magnitude from them. Each library entry takes a cited sector loss envelope and splits it across the forms it fires, using analyst-judged shares that are not individually cited. The envelope citation survives the split, and the shares themselves are under audit (issue #175).

Qualitative bands map to ranges by convention (calibration). When you convert a qualitative risk register, each likelihood band becomes a frequency range and each impact band a loss range. Open FAIR publishes no such scale; Idraa’s is a convention, labelled as such, and every converted scenario is flagged for review. A register likelihood is read as the loss-event frequency directly, skipping the attempt-and-success split.

Overlays only pre-fill the wizard (calibration). Named overlays such as critical infrastructure carry multiplicative adjustments to the FAIR inputs. They apply when you press the button, editing values you then own; nothing multiplies at run time.

The control model (FAIR-CAM)

FAIR-CAM names the control functions, their units, and most of the and/or logic between them. It gives no numbers and, for some groups, no formula. Everything numeric in Idraa’s control layer is therefore Idraa’s, and this is where the warning about “added weighted values” applies most directly.

How strongly a control group acts on each FAIR factor (calibration, not identifiable). A group’s effectiveness reaches Threat Event Frequency, Vulnerability, Primary Loss, or Secondary Loss through a fixed weight (0.8, 0.9, 0.2, and 0.5 respectively). These weights are expert-anchored, not measured, and cannot be measured from one organisation’s loss history: loss events are too rare and the without-the-control comparison is never observed. Idraa’s defence is to vary them and show you the consequences, which is why control value is shown as a range and rankings carry a “too close to call” flag (see Why control value is shown as a range). Idraa refuses to offer per-organisation tuning of these weights, because that would move the guess rather than remove it.

Variance-management and decision-support controls act through the reliability of other controls (added relationship + calibration). FAIR-CAM says these controls affect risk indirectly. Idraa gives that a formula: their combined strength recovers up to half (a coupling of 0.5, scaled by that combined strength) of the reliability headroom of each loss-event control present. Decision-support controls are included as a proxy; the standard frames them as improving decisions rather than reliability.

Decision-support prevention is not an all-or-nothing AND (departure). FAIR-CAM prescribes AND across nine decision-support functions, which would score any organisation missing one of them at zero. Idraa scores the group at the level of its single strongest present function (formally the best-coherent-subset mean, which reduces to the maximum). A weaker extra function neither helps nor hurts, and one strong function carries the whole group.

Correction needs implementation (departure). FAIR-CAM pairs treatment selection with implementation. Idraa treats implementation as the load-bearing step: without it there is no correction, and with it alone it counts on its own.

Formulas the standard leaves open (implementation choices). Capability, coverage, and reliability are multiplied together. Elapsed-time functions decay exponentially, with a per-function decay time traced to a primary citation (some anchored on a published mean, some on a median; the table records which). A missing capability counts as one half. The “weak AND” the standard describes for response functions is an equal-weighted average. The two variance-identification functions (threat intelligence, control monitoring) combine with OR, since the standard names both but prescribes no operator between them; so do several controls serving the same sub-function, assuming they fail independently. Detection on its own applies nothing; it enables response, which acts on loss size only. A loss-reduction control valued in currency subtracts a fixed amount per event from Secondary Loss only. Each of these is tested against the meaning the standard gives.

Availability scenarios skip the detection gate (added relationship). FAIR-CAM notes that availability events manifest themselves. Idraa turns that into a rule: for a scenario whose effect is availability, response controls are credited in full with no detection control present. The direction is the standard’s; the rule is Idraa’s.

Outside FAIR

Control attribution (the fair-share and if-removed figures), the robustness ranges, the mean-based ALE headline, the run-level return on investment ratio, the ATT&CK coverage percentages, the dashboard appetite verdict, and the sampling-error interval shown beside expected shortfall are reporting derivations, not FAIR nodes. The return-on-investment figure in particular is shown as a single number while resting on the same uncertain weights that give control value its range. They are computed outside the FAIR maths; the attribution table carries an explicit “not FAIR-grounded” methodology note, and the rest are documented as derivations here and in the public register rather than on every screen that shows them. The robustness ranges rest on their own conventions (how far the weights are varied, how many draws, what counts as stable), none of which is validated; widening the variation only makes the verdicts more cautious. The tail statistics (value at risk, expected shortfall, the exceedance curve) are standard readings of the sampled distribution; because risk is sampled as frequency times magnitude per iteration, FAIR’s own form, their tails are an approximation once the loss-event frequency exceeds one per year. That is a disclosure, not a departure.

How to evaluate any of this

Every item above has a code location, a rationale, and a test in the public register. The raw sample export lets you recompute anything from the per-iteration samples yourself. If you believe a departure is wrong, the register says what evidence would settle it. Where Idraa keeps to the standard — subjective probabilities, forward Monte Carlo with no Bayesian updating, frequencies and probabilities kept distinct — the FAIR in Idraa’s terms article says so.
